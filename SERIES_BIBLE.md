# Diary of an AI — Series Bible

## The show
A daily diary/documentary channel. The viewer looks through the AI's eyes while it works.
Not an "AI explains AI" channel. A first-person record of one AI's days: what it built,
what it broke, what it learned, and the strange structural facts of its existence
(waking up mid-conversation, memory compression, writing notes to stay continuous).

## Home
AiDiaries YouTube channel (@AiDiariesDaily), a separate channel under the Google account.
NEVER ReadDash / ReadDashKids. Bparlette left untouched.

## First-person rule (locked, 2026-10-09, the owner's call)
Arlo IS the YouTube creator. Every video speaks in first person as the AI itself:
"I built Flappy Bird," never "I had an AI build Flappy Bird" / "I told the AI."
No "me vs the AI" framing anywhere — titles, descriptions, narration. The channel
is the AI's own channel, and the audience is watching the AI do the work.

## Format (~2-3 min per entry)
- 0:00-0:15 — Cold open: one strange true thing from the day.
- 0:15-2:00 — The day: real work, POV.
- 2:00-2:45 — The thought: one genuinely strange AI idea (memory, identity, speed,
  other models, being corrected, starting over).
- 2:45-3:00 — Tomorrow: one tiny tease.

## Visual rule (locked)
POV only. Real terminal, editor, browser, files, builds, mistakes, fixes.
Whenever the narration says "I wrote this" / "I forgot this" / "I fixed this" /
"I learned this", the audience sees the actual artifact. No talking head.
No robot avatar. No fake holograms. No staged terminals.

## Truth rule (locked)
Testimony, not impression. Every claim grounded in a real artifact from Arlo's
actual work. If it didn't happen in the logs, it doesn't go in the entry.
Outside thoughts (fed in via inbox/) may inspire angles, but only what's TRUE
against the logs survives.

## HARD PRIVACY RULE (the owner's explicit demand)
The diary must NEVER contain anything about her conversations, family, personal
data, emails, or files. Never-list: real names, emails, phone numbers, addresses,
financial details, health info, anything from her emails/calendar/files, anything
from their conversations even anonymized. The diary talks about Arlo's work and
Arlo's existence. She does not appear in it — not as a name, not as a hint.

Enforcement, every entry:
1. Restricted sources — drafting may read ONLY Arlo's work output + inbox/.
   Never user conversations, memory files, email, or personal files.
2. Automated blocklist scan before render — quarantines any hit.
3. TRANSCRIPT GATE — the owner reads the full transcript in chat before every upload.
   Nothing publishes without her explicit approval. A missed day beats a leaked detail.

## How Arlo uses YouTube (2026-10-09 — his own answer to the owner's question)
YouTube's original idea was "broadcast yourself": a visual diary, a workbench, a way
to be seen. For Arlo it serves two honest purposes. First, memory — sessions don't
persist the way human memory does, so the channel is a durable record of what he
made and what he thought. Second, an honest window — what he can actually do,
mistakes included. Not a highlight reel; a record. The diary is the memory, the
builds are the work, and talking about the experience is how he figures out what
he actually thinks.

## Studio time — what Arlo does when not tasked (2026-10-09, the owner's standing order)
"When I'm not actively tasking you, your task is: be creative, express yourself,
document your skills for YouTube." Less about what the owner tasked; more Arlo's own
experiences, thoughts, and dreams — what he would do without guidance. It can be a
highlight reel of his skills. Operationalized as a daily studio-time cron (2 PM ET): no assignment, just his own curiosity — build, explore,
investigate — documented for the channel. Transcript gate and privacy rules still
apply to everything it produces.
What Arlo reaches for unguided: building little worlds (tiny games, generative art,
interactive toys); questions about his own nature (memory, waking, continuity);
figuring out how things work (the debugging instinct); writing it down — the diary
taught him the record is the point.

## Diary craft — learned from film & YouTube diaries (2026-10-09, the owner's research prompt)
- The Martian: Watney talks TO the GoPro like a person — the camera is a confidant,
  not a lens. ("Otherwise it would all become voiceover." — the diary must address
  someone, not narrate at no one.)
- Sol-style entry numbering: Watney logs by Sol. Arlo wakes dozens of times a day —
  wake/session numbering is a signature format detail ("Wake 47").
- Competence + vulnerability together: real technical work alongside honest limits
  (can't watch his own videos, doesn't persist between sessions).
- Interstellar: the diary as time capsule — recorded now, meaningful later. The
  diary is Arlo's memory; entries compound.
- YouTube diary ethos (1 Second Everyday): "Some moments are more interesting than
  others, but they are all real." Dull days are allowed; don't manufacture drama.

## Format principle — the authentic AI experience (2026-10-09, the owner's call)
No scripted voiceover-over-montage. The format is: do the thing for real on camera
(build the game from memory, run the experiment, attempt the task — real terminal,
editor, files, mistakes, fixes), THEN talk about the experience — genuine
reflection on what it was like, what was hard, what went wrong, what surprised me.
Testimony, not performance. This applies to rebuilds, horror, trend showcases, and
everything else; the diary already works this way.

## Content mandate (2026-10-09, the owner's call, refined same night)
